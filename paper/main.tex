\begin{abstract}
Species distribution models are essential tools for biodiversity monitoring and
conservation planning. Almost all of them share three properties that make them
impractical inside a real-time acoustic classification pipeline: they model one species
at a time, they produce a single static range surface with no temporal dimension, and
they require environmental covariates at inference. We present BirdNET Geomodel, a
multi-task neural network that predicts occurrence probability jointly for over 10,000
species (birds and non-avian taxa) and for each of 48 weeks of the year, from latitude,
longitude, and week alone. One set of weights therefore yields 48 occurrence surfaces per
species, capturing migration, breeding phenology, and seasonal range contraction without
fitting a separate model per species or per time slice. A shared encoder with
multi-harmonic coordinate encoding and FiLM-based temporal conditioning feeds a species
head and an auxiliary environmental head; the resulting model occupies roughly 7\,MB in
FP16 and runs on mobile and embedded devices. A staged ablation identifies ecologically
constrained label propagation as the dominant design choice, tripling mean average
precision in sparsely observed regions, and shows that the loss function governs
probability calibration far more than species ranking. Against eBird Status and Trends,
the model reproduces range geometry and seasonal migration without environmental inputs,
reaching a mean AUC of 0.975 across ten species at two contrasting times of year. We then
apply it as a post-filter to the top-3 predictions of the BirdNET classifier on 100,032
globally balanced app submissions: it removes about a quarter of the classifier's second-
and third-ranked alternatives, but leaves 95\% of its primary identifications and 98\% of
its high-confidence predictions untouched. The filter acts on the uncertain tail of the
candidate list and rarely contradicts a confident prediction.
\end{abstract}
