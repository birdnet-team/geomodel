\begin{abstract}
Species distribution models are essential tools for biodiversity monitoring and
conservation planning, yet most require environmental covariates at inference time and
model one species at a time, which makes them impractical as a component of a real-time
acoustic classification pipeline. We present BirdNET Geomodel, a multi-task neural
network that predicts weekly occurrence probabilities for over 10,000 species ---
birds as well as non-avian taxa --- from latitude, longitude, and week of year alone.
A shared encoder with multi-harmonic coordinate encoding and FiLM-based temporal
conditioning feeds a species head and an auxiliary environmental head; the resulting
model occupies roughly 7\,MB in FP16 and runs on mobile and embedded devices. A staged
ablation identifies label propagation in environmental space as the dominant design
choice, tripling mean average precision in sparsely observed regions, and shows that the
loss function governs probability calibration far more than species ranking. Compared
against eBird Status and Trends, the model reproduces range geometry and seasonal
migration without environmental inputs, reaching a mean AUC of 0.975 across ten species
at two contrasting times of year. Applied as a post-filter to a globally balanced sample
of BirdNET app submissions, it rejects roughly a quarter of the classifier's second- and
third-ranked candidates but under 5\% of top-1 predictions, and only 2\% of those the
classifier is confident about. The model is therefore best understood not as a range map
but as a plausibility filter: over-inclusive by design, inexpensive enough to run
everywhere, and accurate enough to remove implausible identifications without
contradicting confident ones.
\end{abstract}
